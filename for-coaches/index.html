Run the Good, Better, Best Chant With Your Own Team

Youth football, basketball, a classroom, a swim team. Here's exactly how Ben Johnson runs it, how to make it yours, and three free posters you can print tonight.

Last updated November 7, 2026

Why it works for kids

The chant praises improvement, not winning. "Your good" is a kid's effort, and the rhyme says that effort is the thing that moves. That's why it crossed from an NFL locker room to youth fields across Chicago in a single season (Fox 32), and why it had been a classroom staple for decades before that (who said it).

How Ben Johnson runs it

  1. Only after wins. This is the whole design. If you do it after every game it becomes noise. If you do it only after wins it becomes something the team wants (ESPN).
  2. Game balls first. Johnson hands out game balls and says what he needs to say. The chant is the finale, not the opener (Bleacher Nation).
  3. Coach calls, team answers. One line at a time. Don't say it with them; say it to them and make them give it back.
  4. Loud and short. Four lines, then the break. Under fifteen seconds.
  5. Your own break. "Bears on three" becomes "Wolves on three," "Tigers on three," or whatever you are. At Johnson's high school it was "Big. Green. Machine." (Greensboro Sports)

The script

YouGood, better, best!
TeamGood, better, best!
YouNever let it rest!
TeamNever let it rest!
You'Til your good gets better!
Team'Til your good gets better!
YouAnd your better gets best!
TeamAnd your better gets best!
You[Team] on three. One, two, three…
Team[TEAM]!

Making it stick

  • Explain it once, early in the season. Tell them good is the floor and the chant is a promise to keep going. Then never explain it again; let the ritual do the work.
  • Let a kid lead it. After a few weeks, hand the call to a captain. Rotate it.
  • Use it off the field. A good report card, a hard practice in the rain. Anything earned.
  • Say the caveat. "Never let it rest" is about not coasting, not about never resting. Kids take rhymes literally.

For teachers

The chant is a ready-made growth-mindset lesson. Three ways to use it: as a Monday opener the class says back to you; as a writing prompt ("tell me about a time your good got better"); and as the frame for feedback, where every comment names one "good" and one "better." FAQ

How do you do the good, better, best chant?

The coach calls each of the four lines and the team shouts it back, then the coach says "[Team] on three," counts to three, and everyone yells the team name. Do it only after wins.

Can my youth team use the Bears' chant?

Yes. The words are a traditional proverb with no owner. Just keep the Bears' name, logo and NFL marks off anything you print.

Is there a printable good, better, best poster?

Yes. This page has a free 18×24 locker-room poster, an 8.5×11 printable and a one-page coach's card, all PDF, no sign-up.

Should we do the chant after losses too?

No. The Bears only do it after wins, and that scarcity is what makes it work. After a loss, talk; after a win, chant.
