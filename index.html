The Good, Better, Best Chant: Words, Origin and Meaning

Last updated November 7, 2026

What is the Good, Better, Best chant?

"Good, better, best" is the call-and-response chant the Chicago Bears do in the locker room after every win, led by head coach Ben Johnson. He shouts each line and the players shout it back, then the team breaks on "Bears on three."

The full words (every variant is on the words page):

Good, better, best!
Never let it rest.
'Til your good gets better,
And your better gets best.

Some versions say "until" for "'til" and "your better is best" for "gets best"; Johnson's locker-room version uses "gets" on both lines, per ESPN and Fox 32.

What it means: good is the floor, not the goal. The chant is a promise to keep improving after a win, which is why the team only does it after wins and why players say its message hits hardest after a loss.

Where the chant came from

Ben Johnson brought the chant to Chicago from his own high school team. He played quarterback at A.C. Reynolds High School in Asheville, North Carolina, where head coach Bobby Poss had the Rockets yell it after every win; Reynolds won the 4A state title in Johnson's junior year, per ESPN and Sports Illustrated.

Poss didn't invent it either. He first heard it in the early 1990s as the dean of a Fellowship of Christian Athletes camp, where a speaker named Richard Bell used it to fire up the assembly, and it stuck with him (ESPN). The Reynolds version ended "Big. Green. Machine."; the Bears version ends "Bears on three" (ABC7 Chicago).

The chain, in one line: a 19th-century proverb, to an FCA camp speaker, to a high school coach in Asheville, to his quarterback, to an NFL locker room, to the city of Chicago. The long version is on where it came from.

The proverb behind the chant

The chant is an old English schoolroom rhyme, not a football invention. The most common wording is "Good, better, best. Never let it rest. 'Til your good is better and your better is best."

Who wrote it is unknown. Quote sites almost universally credit St. Jerome, the 4th-century translator of the Latin Bible (Passionists, Quotefancy), but no Jerome text contains it, and a rhyme that only works in modern English can't be a literal 1,600-year-old quotation. It's also wrongly credited to Tim Duncan; readers on one quote site recall learning it in American elementary schools in the 1950s and 1960s and in a Ghanaian primary school in the 1970s, which puts it in circulation long before either name was attached (Values.com comments). A Dallas writer flagged the St. Jerome credit as a "fake motivational quote" when it appeared on a district banner (D Magazine).

So the honest answer: a classic classroom maxim of unknown authorship, in print and in use since at least the mid-20th century, popularly (and probably wrongly) attributed to St. Jerome. The full paper trail, from an 1897 newspaper on, is on who really said it.

Wording variants people search for: "until your good is better" / "'til your good gets better"; "your better is best" / "your better gets best"; "never let it rest" / "may I never rest."

Using the chant with your own team

Coaches, teachers and parents: the chant works for a youth team or a classroom because it praises improvement, not winning. "Your good" is a kid's effort. Here is how Johnson runs it, step by step, so you can copy it:

  1. Do it only after wins. The scarcity is the point.
  2. Hand out game balls first; the chant is the finale, not the opener.
  3. Coach calls each line, team shouts it back; four lines, then your own break ("Bears on three" becomes "Wolves on three").
  4. Keep it loud and short. The whole thing is under 15 seconds.

The saying itself is a public-domain proverb, so put it on your wall, your banner, your team's T-shirts. Just keep team names, logos and NFL marks off anything you print. Free posters, a printable and a one-page coach's card are on the coaches page.

FAQ

What does the Bears' "good, better, best" chant say?

"Good, better, best. Never let it rest. 'Til your good gets better, and your better gets best."

Who started the good, better, best chant?

Bears head coach Ben Johnson, who learned it from his high school coach Bobby Poss at A.C. Reynolds in Asheville, N.C.

When did the Bears start doing it?

After a preseason win over Buffalo on Aug 17, 2025; fans first saw it after the Week 3 win over Dallas on Sep 21, 2025.

Do the Bears do it after every game?

Only after wins.

Did St. Jerome say "good, better, best, never let it rest"?

It's widely attributed to him, but no Jerome text contains it; it's an English schoolroom rhyme of unknown authorship.

Is it "never let it rest" or "don't let it rest"?

The Bears say "never let it rest." Both appear in older versions.

Is it "your good gets better" or "your good is better"?

Johnson's version uses "gets"; the classic proverb uses "is."

Can my team use it?

Yes. The saying is a public-domain proverb; the Bears' name, logos and colors as trademarks are not, so keep those off anything you print.
