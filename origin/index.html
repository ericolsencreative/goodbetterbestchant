Where the Good, Better, Best Chant Came From

Ben Johnson didn't write it. Neither did his high school coach. The chain runs from a 19th-century proverb through a camp speaker, a North Carolina locker room, and a quarterback who became a head coach.

Last updated November 7, 2026

The short version

Ben Johnson learned the chant as a high school quarterback at A.C. Reynolds High School in Asheville, North Carolina, where head coach Bobby Poss had the Rockets yell it after every win. Poss first heard it in the early 1990s at a Fellowship of Christian Athletes camp, from a speaker named Richard Bell. Johnson brought it to Chicago in his first season as Bears head coach, 2025 (ESPN, ABC7 Chicago).

The proverb itself is far older than any of them; see who said it.

Richard Bell and the FCA camp

In the early 1990s Bobby Poss was the dean of a Fellowship of Christian Athletes camp. He asked a speaker, Richard Bell, to get the assembly fired up one night. Bell's answer was the "Good, better, best!" call and response. It worked well enough that Poss kept it (ABC7 / ESPN).

Bobby Poss and the A.C. Reynolds Rockets

Poss made it the Rockets' post-win ritual. After every victory the team gathered and yelled it back to him, ending not with a team name on three but with "Big. Green. Machine." (Greensboro Sports). Johnson played quarterback for Poss, and Reynolds won the North Carolina 4A state title in Johnson's junior year (Sports Illustrated).

Johnson wasn't the only one who carried it out of Asheville. Country singer Chase Rice, a linebacker and co-captain on those Reynolds teams, has said his band gathers for a three-minute chant before every show that starts with "Good, better, best!" (ABC7 / ESPN). Tim Bagamary, now head coach at Eastern Guilford High School in North Carolina, came through the same program and uses it with his Wildcats (Greensboro Sports).

Ben Johnson brings it to Chicago

Johnson was hired as the Bears' head coach in January 2025. After the team's first preseason game under him, a 38–0 win over Buffalo at Soldier Field on August 17, 2025, he gathered the players in the locker room and told them to repeat after him. Some were skeptical of a high school chant in an NFL locker room (ESPN).

The public first saw it after Johnson's first regular-season win, 31–14 over Dallas on September 21, 2025, when the team posted the locker-room video. From then on the Bears' social accounts posted the speech after every win, and the chant spread from the locker room to bars, youth fields and classrooms across Chicago (Chicago Tribune via KDH News, Fox 32).

By the time the Bears beat Green Bay 31–27 in the wild-card round on January 10, 2026, their first playoff win in fifteen years, the chant was a national story (Bleacher Nation). A Chicago hip-hop group released a song built on it (WGN). Johnson brought it back for the 2026 season after the opener in Charlotte (Sun-Times).

Why it stuck

Players have been consistent about this. Defensive end Montez Sweat told ESPN it's simply who Johnson is: never satisfied, telling you good is not good enough (ESPN). Safety Kevin Byard said the message only landed after hearing it over and over: it's basically "never let it rest" (SI). A chant you only get to do after a win is a reward with a warning inside it. That's the whole trick.

The chain, in one line

A 19th-century schoolroom proverb → a Fellowship of Christian Athletes camp speaker named Richard Bell → Bobby Poss and the A.C. Reynolds Rockets → their quarterback, Ben Johnson → the Chicago Bears locker room → the city of Chicago.

FAQ

Who started the good, better, best chant?

Bears head coach Ben Johnson brought it to Chicago. He learned it from his high school coach, Bobby Poss, at A.C. Reynolds High School in Asheville, N.C.

Where did Bobby Poss get it?

From a speaker named Richard Bell at a Fellowship of Christian Athletes camp in the early 1990s, where Poss was the dean.

When did the Bears first do it?

After a 38–0 preseason win over Buffalo on August 17, 2025. Fans first saw it after the Week 3 win over Dallas on September 21, 2025.

Did Ben Johnson invent good, better, best?

No. The rhyme is a traditional proverb that was in print by the 1890s. Johnson's contribution was making it a post-win ritual in an NFL locker room.

Who else uses the chant?

Country singer Chase Rice, Johnson's high school teammate, uses it before shows. Several North Carolina high school programs with A.C. Reynolds roots use it, and youth teams across Chicago adopted it in 2025.
