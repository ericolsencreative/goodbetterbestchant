No. 21 · Week 11 vs Saints, W 21–13

Chicago21
November 22, 2026
Soldier Field, Chicago, IL
New Orleans Saints13
Ben Johnson postgame locker room speech after win over Saints

Video: ChicagoBears.com. Opens on the team's site.

How the Bears won

  • Two first-half takeaways turned into touchdowns: Clark Phillips III forced a CJ Donaldson fumble that Austin Booker recovered, setting up a 3-yard Caleb Williams shovel pass to D'Andre Swift, and rookie Dillon Thieneman intercepted Tyler Shough to set up a 15-yard Williams touchdown to Colston Loveland, who hurdled safety Julian Blackmon on the way in. [source]
  • Chicago led 16–0 in the second quarter before the Saints answered with an Alvin Kamara touchdown on fourth-and-goal and a 56-yard Charlie Smyth field goal at the half to make it 16–10. [source]
  • Montez Sweat sacked Shough in the end zone for a safety early in the fourth quarter to make it 21–10, and the defense later stopped New Orleans on fourth-and-2 at the Bears' 10 with 8:42 left. [source]
  • Jeffery Simmons was the player of the game: three sacks, tying his career high, plus four tackles and six pressures. Per Yahoo, the defense's takeaways produced 13 of the Bears' 21 points. [source]
  • Williams went 15-of-23 for 181 yards, two touchdowns and an interception, a step up from his four-interception night against Tampa Bay. [source]
  • The Bears were 11½-point favorites, their biggest projected margin since a 2009 game against Cleveland, and are 5–0 since Williams returned from his hamstring injury. [source]
  • It was the fifth straight win and moved the Bears to 8–2; they have not trailed in five straight games, their longest such run since 1959. Next: at Detroit on Thanksgiving. [source]

The chant

Chant No. 21 of the Johnson era.

  • The team posted Johnson's locker-room speech on its website within hours of the game, captioned simply "Good. Better. Best." [source]

No. 20 · Week 9 vs Buccaneers, W 27–24

Chicago27
November 8, 2026
Soldier Field, Chicago, IL
Sunday Night Football
Tampa Bay Buccaneers24
"Our best football is yet to come" — Ben Johnson's locker room speech

Video: Chicago Bears on YouTube. Click to play.

How the Bears won

  • Cairo Santos kicked a 38-yard field goal as time expired to win it, after Caleb Williams found Colston Loveland wide open down the left sideline for 38 yards to the Tampa Bay 16 with 38 seconds left. [source]
  • The Bears won despite five turnovers: a career-high four interceptions by Williams plus a Luther Burden III fumble. Williams finished 22-of-37 for 279 yards, two touchdowns and four picks. [source]
  • Chicago led 14–0 after one quarter on a 9-yard Williams-to-Rome Odunze touchdown (Odunze's first of the season) and a 56-yard catch-and-run score by Burden, who finished with seven catches for 97 yards. [source]
  • Three of Williams' interceptions came in the second quarter as Tampa Bay's pressure took over; Zyon McCollum picked him off twice, with Tykee Smith and Alex Anzalone adding one each. [source]
  • Jaylon Johnson's 67-yard interception return for a touchdown in the third quarter, with a Montez Sweat block clearing the path, pushed the lead back to 24–13 and was the play that kept the Bears in front. [source]
  • Baker Mayfield rallied Tampa Bay with a 6-yard Bucky Irving touchdown run and his own two-point scramble to tie it 24–24 with 2:51 left; Mayfield finished 18-of-32 for 243 yards and two interceptions and was sacked four times. [source]
  • The two top rushing teams in the league entering the weekend got little on the ground: Tampa Bay ran for 126 yards, Chicago for 72 on 22 carries with a long of 9. [source]
  • The Bears defense held the Bucs to 2-of-12 on third down, picked Mayfield off twice (Cam Lewis and Jaylon Johnson), and recorded four sacks; Jeffery Simmons had one of the night's loudest moments with a sack of Mayfield. [source]
  • A 92-yard Zavion Thomas kickoff return touchdown was wiped out by a holding penalty on D'Marco Jackson. [source]
  • The win was Chicago's fourth straight and capped a three-game prime-time run (Thursday, Monday, Sunday night); the Bears go into their Week 10 bye at 7–2. [source]
  • Attendance at Soldier Field was 60,470. [source]

The chant

Chant No. 20 of the Johnson era.

Game ball: Jaylon Johnson. See the full tracker.

  • Jaylon Johnson got a game ball for the 67-yard pick-six that put the Bears back up 24–13 in the third quarter, the play that kept the night from getting away. [source]
  • Johnson's postgame message was about survival rather than style: he told reporters it is hard to win with five turnovers and the team found a way to do it anyway, and credited the defense for rising to the occasion. [source]
  • The team's own postgame clips from Sunday night carried the captions "We gotta stay hot" and "Our best football is yet to come." [source]
  • The full player media session from the locker room is on the Bears' channel as a separate video. [source]

No. 19 · Week 8 at Seahawks, W 34–17

Chicago34
November 2, 2026
Lumen Field, Seattle
Monday Night Football
Seattle Seahawks17
Building something special #nfl #mondaynightfootball #chicagobears

Video: Chicago Bears on YouTube. Click to play.

How the Bears won

  • The Bears opened with a 17-play, 80-yard touchdown drive that took 8:32 off the clock and ended in Caleb Williams' 1-yard TD pass to Luther Burden III; Seattle had not allowed an opening-drive touchdown in 16 games. [source]
  • D'Andre Swift ran 27 times for 69 yards and three second-half touchdowns (1, 5 and 5 yards), the first Bear with three rushing TDs in a half since Walter Payton in 1977; his 10 rushing TDs through eight games are the most by a Bear since Rick Casares in 1956. [source]
  • Williams went 24-of-34 for 247 yards and a touchdown with no interceptions (101.0 rating), extending his streak of 36 straight games with 20+ attempts and no multi-interception game, breaking a tie with Aaron Rodgers for the longest such run in NFL history. [source]
  • After Sam Darnold's 7-yard TD to Cooper Kupp and a two-point conversion cut the lead to 20-17 with 11:46 left, the Bears answered with 14 unanswered points. [source]
  • Austin Booker blocked a 33-yard Jason Myers field goal early in the fourth quarter and later tipped a Darnold pass that James Lynch intercepted and returned to the Seattle 6, setting up Swift's third touchdown. [source]
  • Jeffery Simmons, acquired in a trade the previous week, made his Bears debut and tied for the team lead with 7 tackles while adding a sack, two tackles for loss and a pass breakup. [source]
  • Chicago's defense forced four three-and-outs on Seattle's 11 possessions, held the Seahawks to 1-of-11 on third down, and allowed one touchdown on four red-zone trips. [source]
  • The Bears' 34 points, 363 total yards and 22 first downs were all the most Seattle's No. 1-ranked defense had allowed in 2026; the Seahawks had not given up more than 301 yards in any of their first seven games. [source]
  • The 17-point margin was larger than any Seattle loss in its previous 30 games (including the playoffs); the defending champions' six prior losses since the start of 2025 had come by a combined 17 points. [source]
  • Chicago became the first team ever to beat both of the previous season's Super Bowl participants in consecutive prime-time games (Patriots on TNF, Seahawks on MNF), and the win made the Bears 6-2, their best eight-game start since 2012. [source]
  • Rome Odunze had a team-high 87 yards on 4 catches; Colston Loveland (7 for 65) and Burden (7 for 56 and the TD) tied for the catch lead. Cairo Santos was perfect on six kicks, including field goals of 44 and 38 yards. [source]
  • It was the Bears' third straight win and third straight 30-point game (31, 30, 34), their first such streak since 2020, and Johnson's teams have never trailed in the three games since Williams returned. [source]

The chant

Chant No. 19 of the Johnson era.

Game balls: D'Andre Swift, Austin Booker, Jeffery Simmons. See the full tracker.

  • Johnson gave out three game balls: D'Andre Swift (three TDs on 27 carries), Austin Booker (blocked field goal plus the tipped pass that became an interception) and Jeffery Simmons (team-high tackles and a sack in his Bears debut). [source]
  • Johnson told the team the game would be physical and praised them for riding out its ups and downs, then said the Bears 'didn't come here to make a statement' but 'came here to build something, and we're just getting started.' [source]
  • Clips of the speech spread widely, with ML Football calling it an all-time locker room speech and fans praising the Bears' culture; the Bears' own Short of the moment was captioned 'Building something special #mondaynightfootball.' [source]
  • Simmons also addressed the room after receiving his game ball in his first week with the team, per video of the celebration. [source]
  • The full speech is hosted on chicagobears.com as 'Ben Johnson postgame locker room speech after MNF win over Seahawks.' [source]

No. 18 · Week 7 vs Patriots, W 30–24

Chicago30
October 22, 2026
Soldier Field, Chicago
Thursday Night Football
New England Patriots24
Bears postgame locker room after TNF victory vs. Patriots

Video: Chicago Bears on YouTube. Click to play.

How the Bears won

  • Caleb Williams, in his second game back from a hamstring injury, went 22-of-33 for 308 yards and two touchdowns (116.7 rating), following a 305-yard game in Atlanta four days earlier. [source]
  • Luther Burden III caught 4 passes for 130 yards and a TD, all in the first half, including a 58-yard catch that set up D'Andre Swift's 4-yard touchdown run and a 24-yard screen he took to the end zone with 14 seconds left in the half. [source]
  • New England answered Burden's score on the final play of the half, a Drake Maye-to-Rhamondre Stevenson touchdown with no time remaining that cut the Bears' lead to 20-14. [source]
  • Colston Loveland opened the second half with a 12-yard TD catch to finish a 9-play, 72-yard drive and posted a season-high 7 receptions for 69 yards. [source]
  • Cairo Santos missed two 53-yard field goals in the fourth quarter (one off the upright) before drilling a career-long 58-yarder with 1:34 left to make it 30-21. [source]
  • After Andy Borregales' 47-yard field goal made it 30-24 with 37 seconds left, Rome Odunze recovered the onside kick to end it. Odunze had 4 catches for 69 yards, including three 21-yard grabs on the two decisive fourth-quarter drives. [source]
  • Drake Maye was 24-of-31 for 321 yards and two touchdowns with a rushing TD, but D'Marco Jackson picked him off in the end zone for the game's only takeaway, and Kyler Gordon sacked him on fourth-and-5 to force a turnover on downs. [source]
  • Swift led all rushers with 78 yards and a touchdown on 22 carries; New England managed 70 rushing yards on 17 attempts. [source]
  • The win snapped the Patriots' three-game winning streak, moved Chicago to 5-2 (fourth win in five games) and dropped New England to 4-3. [source]
  • Tight end Cole Kmet was carted off with a knee injury midway through the fourth quarter, and rookie cornerback Malik Muhammad left with a toe injury. [source]
  • The game was played four days after the Falcons win on a short week, and the Bears honored Mike Ditka during the Thursday night broadcast. [source]

The chant

Chant No. 18 of the Johnson era.

Game balls: Luther Burden III, Rome Odunze, D'Marco Jackson, Cairo Santos. See the full tracker.

  • Johnson handed out four game balls: Luther Burden III (130 first-half yards and a TD), Rome Odunze (69 second-half yards plus the onside-kick recovery), D'Marco Jackson (6 tackles, a PBU and the game's only interception) and Cairo Santos (career-long 58-yard FG after missing from 53). [source]
  • Santos' game ball came with the subplot that he had adjusted his approach after the two misses, per his postgame comments, and his 58-yarder was the longest of his career. [source]
  • The Good-Better-Best chant closed the speech, and the Bears published the full locker-room video on YouTube as 'Bears postgame locker room after TNF victory vs. Patriots' and on chicagobears.com as 'Ben Johnson postgame locker room speech after win over Patriots.' [source]
  • The Bears' own Shorts from the night were captioned 'Good luck falling asleep after this one #postgame #victory' and 'Luther wanted to put on a show #postgame #victory.' [source]

No. 17 · Week 6 at Falcons, W 31–23

Chicago31
October 18, 2026
Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Atlanta
Atlanta Falcons23
Needed this. #lockerroom #nfl #gameday

Video: Chicago Bears on YouTube. Click to play.

How the Bears won

  • Caleb Williams returned from a hamstring injury that cost him three games and completed his first 12 passes, finishing 30-of-36 for 305 yards, a touchdown, no turnovers and a 111.2 passer rating. [source]
  • Williams hit Colston Loveland for a 14-yard touchdown on the opening drive, and the Bears were 10-for-10 passing for 122 yards across their first two possessions, outgaining Atlanta 154–3 in the first quarter. [source]
  • The opening drive featured a hook-and-lateral on third-and-11: Williams to Rome Odunze, who pitched to Roschon Johnson for a 12-yard gain and a first down. [source]
  • Kyler Gordon, activated from the PUP list the day before the game after a calf injury, made his season debut with three sacks and two forced fumbles, including the strip-sack of Michael Penix Jr. with 2:54 left that Devin Bush recovered to seal the win. [source]
  • D'Andre Swift scored twice on short runs (4 and 2 yards) and Roschon Johnson added a 4-yard TD; Cairo Santos' 50-yard field goal pushed the lead to 31-7 in the third quarter. [source]
  • Special teams produced two takeaways on Zachariah Branch: Josh Blackwell recovered a muffed punt at the Atlanta 10, and Zavier Scott stripped Branch on a kickoff with Nick McCloud recovering at the 31. [source]
  • Atlanta rallied from 31-7 with two Kyle Pitts touchdown catches and two-point conversions to Branch and Drake London, cutting it to 31-23 with 5:41 left after a Roschon Johnson fumble. [source]
  • Luther Burden III led the Bears with 8 catches for 95 yards; Loveland had 6 for 65 and the TD. Penix finished 19-of-33 for 225 yards and three touchdowns. [source]
  • The Bears were flagged 12 times for 133 yards and had receiver Jahdae Walker ejected in the fourth quarter for a personal foul. [source]
  • With Montez Sweat, Dayo Odeyingbo, T.J. Edwards and Tyrique Stevenson out or limited, Chicago rotated out most of its first-string defense while leading 31-7. [source]
  • The win, one week after a 31-7 loss at Green Bay, moved the Bears to 4-2 and dropped Atlanta to 2-4; Johnson called the game 'one of those crossroads.' It also fell on what would have been Mike Ditka's 87th birthday. [source]

The chant

Chant No. 17 of the Johnson era.

Game balls: Caleb Williams, Kyler Gordon, Grady Jarrett. See the full tracker.

  • Johnson gave game balls in the locker room to Caleb Williams (305 yards in his return) and Kyler Gordon (three sacks, two forced fumbles in his first game action in roughly nine months). [source]
  • A third game ball went, on the team plane, to defensive tackle Grady Jarrett, an Atlanta-area native who spent his first 10 NFL seasons with the Falcons. [source]
  • In the speech Johnson credited all three phases rather than singling anyone out, saying the plan required offense, defense and special teams to contribute and that the team's identity and play style had to show up after the Packers loss. [source]
  • Williams addressed the room about the weeks he spent sidelined, thanked teammates across units and closed with 'It's Bears over everybody.' [source]
  • Johnson punctuated the Good-Better-Best chant with a leg-shaking celebration dance in the visiting locker room, which the Sun-Times described as the coach being 'back in his bag.' [source]
  • The team posted the full speech on chicagobears.com as 'Ben Johnson postgame locker room speech after win over Falcons' with the description 'Good. Better. Best.' [source]

No. 16 · Week 4 vs Jets, W 23–12

Chicago23
October 4, 2026
Soldier Field, Chicago
New York Jets12
Bears postgame locker room after Week 4 victory vs. Jets

Video: Chicago Bears on YouTube. Click to play.

How the Bears won

  • Tyson Bagent started in place of the injured Caleb Williams (hamstring) and went 25-of-34 for a career-high 268 yards with one interception in his first start since 2023; he became the third different QB to win a Bears start in the first four weeks (after Williams and Case Keenum). [source]
  • Kyle Monangai carried a career-high 30 times for 146 yards and both Bears touchdowns: a 1-yard plunge on fourth-and-goal in the first quarter and a 5-yard run in the third that capped a 9-play, 72-yard drive. [source]
  • The Bears outgained the Jets 492–157, piled up 34 first downs to New York's 7, never punted, and held the ball for 42:45 to the Jets' 17:15. [source]
  • Chicago ran 89 offensive plays to New York's 32; per ESPN's Benjamin Solak, a play differential that large had happened only three other times since 1970 and not since 1999. [source]
  • Dennis Allen's defense held the Jets to 0-for-7 on third down, sacked Geno Smith three times with seven QB hits, and allowed just one touchdown for the second straight game: Smith's 58-yard strike to Isaiah Williams in the fourth quarter (the PAT was missed). [source]
  • Two apparent D'Andre Swift touchdown runs were overturned on replay, one of them creating a turnover on downs at the goal line, and the Bears finished only 2-for-7 scoring TDs in the red zone, leaning on three Cairo Santos field goals (30, 30, 21). [source]
  • The Bears gave the ball away twice: Minkah Fitzpatrick picked off a Bagent pass intended for Colston Loveland (the Jets' first interception since the 2024 finale), and Jamien Sherwood forced a Swift fumble recovered by Brandon Stephens. The defense held New York to a field goal after each. [source]
  • Chicago closed the game with an 18-play, 76-yard drive that burned 9:07 off the clock, aided by three Jets defensive penalties that converted third or fourth downs. [source]
  • Rome Odunze's 37-yard catch on third-and-5 set up a Santos field goal and was his best outing of the season to that point with Bagent at QB. [source]
  • Monangai left with a thumb injury that Johnson called day-to-day; he also dealt with a toe injury and would miss the following week at Green Bay. [source]
  • Three Jets defenders (Dane Belton, Minkah Fitzpatrick, Darrell Jackson) were later fined a combined $43,983 by the NFL for illegal hits in the game; no Bears were fined. [source]
  • The win moved the Bears to 3-1 and made them 2-0 without Caleb Williams; it came six days after their 27-7 Monday night win over the Eagles. [source]

The chant

Chant No. 16 of the Johnson era.

Game ball: Devin Bush. See the full tracker.

  • Johnson handed out one game ball, to linebacker Devin Bush, for a line of four tackles, a tackle for loss, a sack and a QB hit against a Jets offense held to 157 yards. [source]
  • Johnson closed the speech by asking the room twice, 'You guys know what next week is?' then 'Alright, enough said. Bring it in,' a nod to the upcoming trip to Lambeau Field without naming the Packers, before the Good-Better-Best chant. [source]
  • The team's official locker-room video was posted as 'Bears postgame locker room after Week 4 victory vs. Jets' on the Chicago Bears YouTube channel and as 'Ben Johnson postgame locker room speech after win over Jets' on chicagobears.com. [source]

No. 15 · Week 3 vs Eagles, W 27–7

Chicago27
September 28, 2026
Soldier Field, Chicago
Monday Night Football
Philadelphia Eagles7
Bears postgame locker room after Week 3 victory vs. Eagles

Video: Chicago Bears on YouTube. Click to play.

How the Bears won

  • Third-string quarterback Case Keenum, 38, started because Caleb Williams was out with a hamstring injury and backup Tyson Bagent had only cleared concussion protocol the day before; it was Keenum's first regular-season action since 2023. [source]
  • Keenum went 24-of-34 for 247 yards and two touchdowns (110.8 rating) and added a 1-yard sneak for a third score; it was his 67th career start. [source]
  • With the win Keenum joined Ryan Fitzpatrick as the only quarterbacks to win a start for seven different NFL franchises. [source]
  • Chicago opened with an 11-play, 66-yard drive capped by an 8-yard shovel-pass touchdown to Luther Burden III, who was pushed across by left tackle Braxton Jones. [source]
  • Philadelphia's only points came on a Jalen Hurts tush-push touchdown as the first half expired, cutting it to 10–7; the Eagles never led. [source]
  • The defense forced three takeaways: Austin Booker's 'Peanut Punch' fumble of Tank Bigsby recovered by Xavier Woods, rookie Dillon Thieneman's first career interception of Andy Dalton on third-and-goal from the 2, and a T.J. Edwards interception of Hurts on fourth-and-5. [source]
  • Hurts was briefly pulled for a concussion evaluation after a hit from D'Marco Jackson, returned, and finished 16-of-25 for 153 yards with an interception and a 64.2 rating. [source]
  • Keenum's 41-yard touchdown to Kalif Raymond (6 catches, 90 yards) made it 20–7 in the third quarter after the Eagles opened the half with three straight three-and-outs. [source]
  • The Bears outgained Philadelphia 375–248, held the ball for 36:53, and limited the Eagles to 2-of-9 on third down and 62 total yards in the second half. [source]
  • A Colston Loveland touchdown catch was overturned on replay (ruled down at the 1), and Chicago came away empty on that trip after a fourth-down pass to Burden was broken up by Quinyon Mitchell. [source]
  • The win moved the Bears to 2–1, bouncing back from a 9–3 loss to Minnesota, and handed the previously unbeaten Eagles their first loss of the season (also 2–1). [source]

The chant

Chant No. 15 of the Johnson era.

Game balls: Dennis Allen and the defense, Case Keenum. See the full tracker.

  • Johnson gave out two game balls: one to defensive coordinator Dennis Allen and the entire defense (saying he couldn't single out one player after three takeaways) and one to Case Keenum. [source]
  • Handed the game ball and the floor, Keenum spoke about a career of opportunities taken away and how Johnson talked him out of retirement during a planned 15-minute conversation that ran about two and a half hours, closing with 'I may not be a cowboy, but this ain't my first rodeo.' [source]
  • Caleb Williams and Tyson Bagent stood watching as Keenum addressed the room; Johnson told reporters the night was 'all about Case Keenum and what he was able to do.' [source]
  • The team's social channels released the speech video, including the Good, Better, Best finale, and the Bears also posted a 'Ben Johnson postgame locker room speech after Monday night win over Eagles' clip on chicagobears.com. [source]

No. 14 · Week 1 at Panthers, W 59–37

Chicago59
September 13, 2026
Bank of America Stadium, Charlotte
Season opener
Carolina Panthers37
Postgame locker room speech vs. Panthers | Chicago Bears

Video: Chicago Bears on YouTube. Click to play.

How the Bears won

  • The 96 combined points made it the highest-scoring season opener in NFL history, eight more than the previous mark of 88 (Tampa Bay 48, New Orleans 40 in 2018). [source]
  • The 59 points were a Bears franchise record for an opener (previous: 45 vs. Green Bay in 1948) and the fourth-most in team history; it was also the most points Carolina has ever allowed. [source]
  • Chicago led 31–24 at halftime after Caleb Williams threw for 196 first-half yards, including a 22-yard touchdown to Cole Kmet. [source]
  • Carolina tied it 31–31 on Chuba Hubbard's second touchdown before the Bears pulled away with four unanswered scores. [source]
  • Caleb Williams totaled four touchdowns: 269 passing yards with two TD passes plus 65 rushing yards and two rushing scores. [source]
  • D'Andre Swift, fresh off a $33.75 million extension, ran 18 times for 124 yards and three touchdowns. [source]
  • Kyle Monangai added 100 rushing yards, highlighted by a 61-yard touchdown run that made it 45–31. [source]
  • A Tetairoa McMillan fumble set up Williams' touchdown pass to Jahdae Walker for a 52–31 lead with 6:27 left. [source]
  • Bryce Young threw for 361 yards and three touchdowns and ran for another; Jalen Coker had eight catches for a career-high 138 yards and two scores. [source]
  • Left tackle Braxton Jones (upper body) and cornerback Tyrique Stevenson (shoulder) left the game with third-quarter injuries. [source]

The chant

Chant No. 14 of the Johnson era and the first of the 2026 season.

Game balls: Dillon Thieneman, The offensive line, D'Andre Swift. See the full tracker.

  • This was the first Good, Better, Best of the 2026 season; Johnson said beforehand that nothing had discouraged him from bringing it back, calling it 'a great message' and 'a good rallying cry' that the players enjoy. [source]
  • Johnson admitted the game 'was not pretty for 60 minutes' but said the team weathered the early storm and found its footing, and that the goal was simply to get on the plane with a win. [source]
  • Game balls went to rookie safety Dillon Thieneman, the offensive line (accepted by guard Jonah Jackson on the unit's behalf) and running back D'Andre Swift; Johnson also gave a shout-out to the play-caller. [source]
  • Johnson pointed to the three defensive takeaways as the pregame emphasis coming true, and did not go shirtless this time. [source]
  • After Swift's brief speech, an unidentified player shouted 'I need it!' just before Johnson launched into the chant to break the huddle. [source]
