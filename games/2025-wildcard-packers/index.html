How the Bears won

  • Green Bay scored touchdowns on its first three possessions (Romeo Doubs, Christian Watson and Jayden Reed) and led 21–3 at halftime; Jordan Love, back from the concussion he suffered in Week 16, threw for 139 first-half yards. [source]
  • The Bears trailed 21–6 entering the fourth quarter and were down by as many as 18 points before outscoring the Packers 25–6 in the final period. [source]
  • After a 5-yard D'Andre Swift touchdown run and a Matthew Golden 23-yard score for Green Bay (with a missed extra point), Caleb Williams hit Olamide Zaccheaus for an 8-yard TD and Colston Loveland for the two-point conversion to make it 27–24 with 4:18 left. [source]
  • Brandon McManus missed a 44-yard field goal wide right on the Packers' next drive, and Williams answered with a 25-yard go-ahead touchdown to DJ Moore with 1:43 to play. [source]
  • On Green Bay's final drive, Love dropped a snap on third down at the Chicago 28 and his last-gasp throw into the end zone was knocked away by Jaquan Brisker to end the game. [source]
  • Williams went 24-of-48 for 361 yards, two touchdowns and two interceptions (including a Ty'Ron Hopper pick near the goal line in the third quarter); Love threw for 323 yards and four scores. [source]
  • Rookie tight end Colston Loveland set a career high with eight catches for 137 yards on 15 targets and added the key two-point conversion. [source]
  • The Bears went for it on fourth down six times, converting two, including a fourth-and-2 from their own 38 to Moore during the rally. [source]
  • The defense forced four straight punts to open the second half after allowing 231 first-half yards, with Kevin Byard crediting better pressure and disguised coverages. [source]
  • It was Chicago's first playoff victory since the 2010 season's divisional win over Seattle (January 2011), snapping a three-game postseason losing streak that began with the 2010 NFC Championship loss to Green Bay. [source]
  • The 18-point comeback was the Bears' seventh fourth-quarter comeback win of the season; linebacker T.J. Edwards suffered a season-ending broken fibula in the game. [source]
  • Johnson and Matt LaFleur exchanged only a brief drive-by handshake at midfield; the Bears advanced to host a divisional-round game the following weekend. [source]

The chant after this one

Every win ends the same way: Johnson gathers the room, calls for quiet, and leads the four lines one at a time with the whole team answering. This was chant No. 13 of the Johnson era.

  • Johnson opened his speech by shouting an expletive at the Packers and saying he hated 'those guys'; the team posted the roughly five-minute, bleeped video on social media and it went viral. [source]
  • Game balls went to tight end Colston Loveland (8 catches, 137 yards), defensive end Montez Sweat (second-half pressure on Love) and Caleb Williams, whom Johnson called 'straight ice' when it mattered. [source]
  • Williams then surprised Johnson with a game ball of his own, choking up as he called the coach 'monumental' in his life and honored Johnson's first playoff win as Bears head coach before flipping him the ball and hugging him. [source]
  • Johnson closed by telling players they would never forget the win and leading a raucous Good, Better, Best; the team's accompanying post read 'Not. Done. Yet.' [source]
  • Two days later Johnson refused to apologize, saying 'I don't like that team,' that Green Bay needs to be a real rivalry for Chicago, and that chairman George McCaskey was on the same page; his only regret was a pregame scuffle. [source]
