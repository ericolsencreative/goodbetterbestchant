How the Bears won

  • Third-string quarterback Case Keenum, 38, started because Caleb Williams was out with a hamstring injury and backup Tyson Bagent had only cleared concussion protocol the day before; it was Keenum's first regular-season action since 2023. [source]
  • Keenum went 24-of-34 for 247 yards and two touchdowns (110.8 rating) and added a 1-yard sneak for a third score; it was his 67th career start. [source]
  • With the win Keenum joined Ryan Fitzpatrick as the only quarterbacks to win a start for seven different NFL franchises. [source]
  • Chicago opened with an 11-play, 66-yard drive capped by an 8-yard shovel-pass touchdown to Luther Burden III, who was pushed across by left tackle Braxton Jones. [source]
  • Philadelphia's only points came on a Jalen Hurts tush-push touchdown as the first half expired, cutting it to 10–7; the Eagles never led. [source]
  • The defense forced three takeaways: Austin Booker's 'Peanut Punch' fumble of Tank Bigsby recovered by Xavier Woods, rookie Dillon Thieneman's first career interception of Andy Dalton on third-and-goal from the 2, and a T.J. Edwards interception of Hurts on fourth-and-5. [source]
  • Hurts was briefly pulled for a concussion evaluation after a hit from D'Marco Jackson, returned, and finished 16-of-25 for 153 yards with an interception and a 64.2 rating. [source]
  • Keenum's 41-yard touchdown to Kalif Raymond (6 catches, 90 yards) made it 20–7 in the third quarter after the Eagles opened the half with three straight three-and-outs. [source]
  • The Bears outgained Philadelphia 375–248, held the ball for 36:53, and limited the Eagles to 2-of-9 on third down and 62 total yards in the second half. [source]
  • A Colston Loveland touchdown catch was overturned on replay (ruled down at the 1), and Chicago came away empty on that trip after a fourth-down pass to Burden was broken up by Quinyon Mitchell. [source]
  • The win moved the Bears to 2–1, bouncing back from a 9–3 loss to Minnesota, and handed the previously unbeaten Eagles their first loss of the season (also 2–1). [source]

The chant after this one

Every win ends the same way: Johnson gathers the room, calls for quiet, and leads the four lines one at a time with the whole team answering. This was chant No. 15 of the Johnson era.

Game balls: Dennis Allen and the defense, Case Keenum. See the full tracker.

  • Johnson gave out two game balls: one to defensive coordinator Dennis Allen and the entire defense (saying he couldn't single out one player after three takeaways) and one to Case Keenum. [source]
  • Handed the game ball and the floor, Keenum spoke about a career of opportunities taken away and how Johnson talked him out of retirement during a planned 15-minute conversation that ran about two and a half hours, closing with 'I may not be a cowboy, but this ain't my first rodeo.' [source]
  • Caleb Williams and Tyson Bagent stood watching as Keenum addressed the room; Johnson told reporters the night was 'all about Case Keenum and what he was able to do.' [source]
  • The team's social channels released the speech video, including the Good, Better, Best finale, and the Bears also posted a 'Ben Johnson postgame locker room speech after Monday night win over Eagles' clip on chicagobears.com. [source]
