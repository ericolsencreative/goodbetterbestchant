The 2025 season, chant by chant

Ben Johnson's first year: 13 chants, from a preseason shutout to a playoff comeback.

Last updated November 15, 2026

13 wins, 13 chants in 2025: 8 at home and 5 on the road. 8 were decided by one score. In the 11 regular-season wins the Bears outscored opponents 305–217.

This was Ben Johnson's first season as head coach, and the season the chant went from a locker-room ritual to something the whole city yelled. It started after a preseason shutout of Buffalo, went public after the Week 3 win over Dallas, and peaked with the shirtless chant in Philadelphia and the Wild Card comeback over Green Bay, the Bears' first playoff win since January 2011. The regular season finished 11–6.

No. 13 · Wild Card vs Packers, W 31–27

Chicago31
January 10, 2026
Soldier Field, Chicago
NFC Wild Card, Saturday
Green Bay Packers27
Bears postgame locker room | Wild Card Round

Video: Chicago Bears on YouTube. Click to play.

How the Bears won

  • Green Bay scored touchdowns on its first three possessions (Romeo Doubs, Christian Watson and Jayden Reed) and led 21–3 at halftime; Jordan Love, back from the concussion he suffered in Week 16, threw for 139 first-half yards. [source]
  • The Bears trailed 21–6 entering the fourth quarter and were down by as many as 18 points before outscoring the Packers 25–6 in the final period. [source]
  • After a 5-yard D'Andre Swift touchdown run and a Matthew Golden 23-yard score for Green Bay (with a missed extra point), Caleb Williams hit Olamide Zaccheaus for an 8-yard TD and Colston Loveland for the two-point conversion to make it 27–24 with 4:18 left. [source]
  • Brandon McManus missed a 44-yard field goal wide right on the Packers' next drive, and Williams answered with a 25-yard go-ahead touchdown to DJ Moore with 1:43 to play. [source]
  • On Green Bay's final drive, Love dropped a snap on third down at the Chicago 28 and his last-gasp throw into the end zone was knocked away by Jaquan Brisker to end the game. [source]
  • Williams went 24-of-48 for 361 yards, two touchdowns and two interceptions (including a Ty'Ron Hopper pick near the goal line in the third quarter); Love threw for 323 yards and four scores. [source]
  • Rookie tight end Colston Loveland set a career high with eight catches for 137 yards on 15 targets and added the key two-point conversion. [source]
  • The Bears went for it on fourth down six times, converting two, including a fourth-and-2 from their own 38 to Moore during the rally. [source]
  • The defense forced four straight punts to open the second half after allowing 231 first-half yards, with Kevin Byard crediting better pressure and disguised coverages. [source]
  • It was Chicago's first playoff victory since the 2010 season's divisional win over Seattle (January 2011), snapping a three-game postseason losing streak that began with the 2010 NFC Championship loss to Green Bay. [source]
  • The 18-point comeback was the Bears' seventh fourth-quarter comeback win of the season; linebacker T.J. Edwards suffered a season-ending broken fibula in the game. [source]
  • Johnson and Matt LaFleur exchanged only a brief drive-by handshake at midfield; the Bears advanced to host a divisional-round game the following weekend. [source]

The chant

Chant No. 13 of the Johnson era.

Game balls: Colston Loveland, Montez Sweat, Caleb Williams. See the full tracker.

  • Johnson opened his speech by shouting an expletive at the Packers and saying he hated 'those guys'; the team posted the roughly five-minute, bleeped video on social media and it went viral. [source]
  • Game balls went to tight end Colston Loveland (8 catches, 137 yards), defensive end Montez Sweat (second-half pressure on Love) and Caleb Williams, whom Johnson called 'straight ice' when it mattered. [source]
  • Williams then surprised Johnson with a game ball of his own, choking up as he called the coach 'monumental' in his life and honored Johnson's first playoff win as Bears head coach before flipping him the ball and hugging him. [source]
  • Johnson closed by telling players they would never forget the win and leading a raucous Good, Better, Best; the team's accompanying post read 'Not. Done. Yet.' [source]
  • Two days later Johnson refused to apologize, saying 'I don't like that team,' that Green Bay needs to be a real rivalry for Chicago, and that chairman George McCaskey was on the same page; his only regret was a pregame scuffle. [source]

No. 12 · Week 16 vs Packers, W 22–16 (OT)

Chicago22
December 20, 2025
Soldier Field, Chicago
Saturday Night (NFC North showdown)
Green Bay Packers16
Bears postgame locker room | 2025 Week 16

Video: Chicago Bears on YouTube. Click to play.

How the Bears won

  • Green Bay led 6–0 at halftime on two Brandon McManus field goals and 13–3 after three quarters following a 33-yard Malik Willis-to-Romeo Doubs touchdown. [source]
  • Packers QB Jordan Love left in the second quarter with a concussion after a helmet-to-helmet hit from Bears rookie Austin Booker; Malik Willis replaced him and went 9-of-11 for 121 yards and a TD. [source]
  • Trailing 16–6 with about five minutes left, the Bears scored 10 points in the final 1:59 of regulation: a 43-yard Cairo Santos field goal, a Josh Blackwell onside-kick recovery at the Chicago 47, and a 6-yard Caleb Williams touchdown pass to Jahdae Walker on fourth-and-4 with 24 seconds left. [source]
  • In overtime Green Bay drove to the Chicago 36, but a botched exchange between Willis and Sean Rhyan on fourth-and-1 ended the drive; Williams then hit DJ Moore for a 46-yard walk-off touchdown. [source]
  • Williams finished 19-of-34 for 250 yards and two touchdowns with no turnovers; both scores came on the Bears' final two possessions. [source]
  • Cairo Santos went 3-for-3 on field goals (46, 51, 43 yards) in swirling wind, and DJ Moore had five catches for 97 yards plus the game-winner. [source]
  • The Bears played without wide receivers Rome Odunze and Luther Burden III, both inactive with injuries. [source]
  • It was Chicago's sixth win of the season after trailing in the final two minutes of regulation, the most in the NFL that year and a franchise record. [source]
  • Williams tied Bo Nix's NFL record for most fourth-quarter comeback wins by a quarterback in his first two seasons (eight). [source]
  • The win improved the Bears to 11–4, clinching their first winning season since 2018 and giving them a game-and-a-half lead over Green Bay (9–5–1) in the NFC North. [source]

The chant

Chant No. 12 of the Johnson era.

Game balls: Cairo Santos, Jahdae Walker, Josh Blackwell, DJ Moore. See the full tracker.

  • Johnson handed out four game balls: kicker Cairo Santos (3-for-3 in the wind), wide receiver Jahdae Walker (game-tying TD while filling in for injured starters), cornerback Josh Blackwell (onside-kick recovery) and DJ Moore (46-yard OT winner, his second game ball in two weeks). [source]
  • Johnson told the room 'this is a special group' and said he had never been around a team that doesn't bat an eye when games get late and tight. [source]
  • The team-posted celebration video was widely shared under the chant's 'Never let it rest' line, with the Bears having just secured their first winning record since 2018. [source]
  • The Bears also posted a short clip of players dancing in the locker room, captioned around having 'thawed out' in time to celebrate the cold-weather win. [source]

How the Bears won

  • Kickoff temperature was 8 degrees with a wind chill of minus-2, making it one of the coldest games in Soldier Field history. [source]
  • The Bears jumped out 14–0 in the first quarter on a 6-yard D'Andre Swift touchdown run and a 3-yard Caleb Williams-to-DJ Moore scoring pass. [source]
  • Cleveland's only points came on a 50-yard Andre Szmyt field goal early in the second half; the Bears then scored the game's final 17 points. [source]
  • Chicago's defense picked off rookie QB Shedeur Sanders three times in the second half (D'Marco Jackson, Jaylon Johnson in the end zone, and C.J. Gardner-Johnson with a 23-yard return), recorded five sacks, and did not allow a touchdown. [source]
  • Caleb Williams finished 17-of-28 for 242 yards and two touchdowns with no turnovers (112.5 rating); Sanders posted a 30.3 passer rating. [source]
  • D'Andre Swift ran 18 times for 98 yards and two scores, including a 17-yard touchdown late in the third quarter in which he shed two tacklers. [source]
  • DJ Moore caught both of Williams' touchdown passes, finishing with four catches for 69 yards. [source]
  • The Bears outgained Cleveland 361–192 and held the Browns to 50 rushing yards. [source]
  • The 28-point margin was Chicago's most lopsided win since a 36–7 victory over Houston on Dec. 13, 2020, and it was the Bears' first 10-win season since 2018. [source]
  • The win moved the Bears to 10–4 and, combined with Green Bay's loss to Denver that day, back into first place in the NFC North ahead of a Week 16 showdown with the Packers. [source]

  • Johnson called Jackson's interception a game-changer that sparked the team in the second half, and praised the defense's performance as outstanding. [source]

  • The Bears published both a locker-room celebration video and a Johnson speech video, plus a photo gallery from the postgame celebration, on chicagobears.com. [source]

No. 10 · Week 13 at Eagles, W 24–15

Chicago24

Philadelphia Eagles15

How the Bears won

  • Chicago ran for 281 yards against the defending Super Bowl champions, with Kyle Monangai (22 carries, 130 yards, TD) and D'Andre Swift (18 carries, 125 yards, TD) both topping 100 — the first time two Bears backs did so in one game since Walter Payton and Matt Suhey on Nov. 10, 1985. [source]
  • Swift was the first running back to reach 100 rushing yards against Philadelphia all season; Chicago had 142 rushing yards by halftime. [source]
  • Trailing 10-9 after Jalen Hurts' 33-yard TD to A.J. Brown (Jake Elliott missed the extra point), the Bears got a Hurts fumble deep in their own territory late in the third quarter, then drove for Monangai's 4-yard TD — a drive that opened with a 31-yard Monangai run and included a fourth-down completion to Colston Loveland. [source]
  • Caleb Williams' 28-yard touchdown to Cole Kmet made it 24-9; Williams finished 17-of-36 for 154 yards and a TD, while Swift's 3-yard run had opened the scoring. [source]
  • Kevin Byard's diving third-quarter interception of a Hurts pass to Brown was his sixth of the season, the most in the NFL and his most since 2017; Nahshon Wright forced a fumble on an Eagles tush-push attempt. [source]
  • Philadelphia managed just two first downs and 83 yards on 17 first-half plays, finished 4-of-12 on third down, and was outgained in snaps 85-51 and first downs 28-14. [source]
  • Hurts threw for 230 yards and two TDs, both to Brown (10 catches, 132 yards), the second a 4-yarder late in the fourth quarter followed by a failed two-point try; Saquon Barkley was held to 56 rushing yards. [source]
  • Chicago played without its top four linebackers, with coordinator Dennis Allen leaning on dime packages and blitzes from Jaquan Brisker and C.J. Gardner-Johnson; Jaylon Johnson and Kyler Gordon returned from injuries. [source]
  • Johnson credited the win to the offensive line of Ozzy Trapilo (second straight start at left tackle), Joe Thuney, Drew Dalman, Jonah Jackson and Darnell Wright, saying the 281-yard day 'doesn't happen without that offensive line.' [source]
  • The fifth straight win moved Chicago to 9-3 and sole possession of first place in the NFC North — one year after a Thanksgiving loss at Detroit had led to Matt Eberflus' firing during a 10-game skid. [source]

The chant

Chant No. 10 of the Johnson era.

  • Mid-speech, Johnson pulled his shirt off over his head and flexed like a pro wrestler as players erupted around him, then led the 'Good, Better, Best' chant bare-chested — believed to be the first time he had done so with the team. [source]
  • The stunt paid off a standing offer from Chicago hot dog stand The Wieners Circle, which had posted in late October that it would give away free hot dogs if Johnson ever went topless in a locker-room speech; Johnson had acknowledged the post at an Oct. 24 press conference, and the stand honored it with a free-hot-dog day the following Tuesday. [source]
  • Caleb Williams confirmed it to reporters — 'He did take his shirt off' — and called his coach 'a man of the people.' [source]
  • Johnson said afterward the players felt good about beating a good opponent on the road and that the staff and players believe in what the team is doing; on Monday he called the shirt removal 'a spur-of-the-moment deal,' joked he wanted to do it before spending any more time away from the weight room, and said his 2-year-old pointed at the TV saying 'No shirt.' [source]
  • NFL on Prime Video posted the clip on X the night of the game ('Come for the Chicago Bears victory speech, stay for Ben Johnson going tops off'), and the Bears' own channel titled its speech clip 'GOOD. BETTER. BEST. #lockerroom #victory #speech' alongside a 'Black Friday W!' postgame short. [source]

How the Bears won

  • Rudolph, starting for the injured Aaron Rodgers, cut it to 31-28 with a 3-yard TD to Pat Freiermuth with 6:27 left; the defense then stuffed Connor Heyward on a fourth-and-1 tush push at the Chicago 30 and Jaquan Brisker batted away Rudolph's fourth-and-6 pass with 17 seconds left. [source]

  • Pittsburgh's first-quarter score came on a 6-yard DK Metcalf run capping a 13-play, 95-yard drive; a 55-yard Kenneth Gainwell run off a tush push set up Jaylen Warren's 1-yard TD late in the first half. [source]
  • The fourth straight win (eighth in nine games) moved Chicago to 8-3, alone atop the NFC North ahead of Green Bay (7-3-1) and Detroit (7-4) — and for once did not require a last-minute comeback. [source]

No. 5 · Week 7 vs Saints, W 26–14

Chicago26
October 19, 2025
Soldier Field, Chicago, IL
New Orleans Saints14
Quiet, our show is on 🍿 #lockerroom #speech #goodbetterbest

Video: Chicago Bears on YouTube. Click to play.

How the Bears won

  • The Bears won their fourth straight game to improve to 4-2 — their longest winning streak since the 2018 NFC North champions closed the regular season with four straight wins. [source]
  • Chicago snapped an eight-game losing streak to New Orleans; the Bears had not beaten the Saints since 2008. [source]
  • Defensive coordinator Dennis Allen faced the Saints for the first time since they fired him as head coach in November 2024; he had spent 15 of the prior 20 seasons in New Orleans. [source]
  • Allen's defense forced four takeaways (three interceptions) and recorded four sacks, giving Chicago 16 takeaways on the season and three-plus in four straight games. [source]
  • Montez Sweat's early strip-sack set up the opening field goal, Nahshon Wright's long interception return set up the first touchdown, and Tremaine Edmunds — who also had a sack — sealed it with a fourth-quarter interception of a tipped pass. [source]
  • The Bears ran for 222 yards: D'Andre Swift posted a season-high 124 yards and a touchdown (his second straight 100-yard game) and rookie Kyle Monangai added a career-high 81 yards and a score. [source]
  • Caleb Williams had a quiet day — 15-of-26 for 172 yards and an interception — and admitted afterward the win came without his best effort. [source]
  • Saints QB Spencer Rattler threw three interceptions (20-of-32, 233 yards); Chris Olave caught both New Orleans touchdowns (5 catches, 98 yards) as the Saints fell to 1-6, their worst start since 1999. [source]
  • Jake Moody, filling in for Cairo Santos for a second straight week, kicked field goals including a 33-yarder that made it 26-14; a Saints false start on a fourth-down goal-line snap had forced the Bears to settle for three earlier. [source]
  • Jaquan Brisker and Kyler Gordon each notched their first sacks of the season on the same drive; CB Tyrique Stevenson (shoulder) and TE Cole Kmet (back) left the game. [source]

The chant

Chant No. 5 of the Johnson era.

Game ball: Dennis Allen. See the full tracker.

  • Johnson ticked off the defense's numbers — four takeaways, four sacks, 44 rushing yards allowed — then announced he had only one game ball and shouted 'Dennis Allen, come here!' Players swarmed Allen, who danced in the middle of his defense. [source]
  • With players chanting 'speech!', Allen told the room 'sometimes you ain't good enough for somewhere else — that's perfectly fine, I love being here,' and closed with 'four ain't enough! Moving on to the next one!' [source]

  • Allen had publicly downplayed the matchup as 'another game' all week, but Kevin Byard said it clearly meant a lot to him and credited Allen's game plan for putting Brisker and Gordon in position to make plays. [source]

How the Bears won

  • Jake Moody, promoted from the practice squad that afternoon to replace the injured Cairo Santos (quad), drilled a 38-yard field goal as time expired for the win in his Bears debut — less than eight hours after joining the active roster. [source]
  • Moody, cut by San Francisco after Week 1 following a miss and a block, went 4-of-5 on the night (47, 48, 41 and 38 yards) with one attempt blocked by Daron Payne. [source]
  • The game came one year after Washington's 'Fail Mary' Hail Mary beat Chicago on the same field; this time Caleb Williams took a knee to bleed the clock to three seconds before the kick, leaving no time for a reply. [source]
  • Trailing 24-22 with 3:10 left, the Bears got the ball when Jayden Daniels botched a third-and-1 handoff and Nahshon Wright recovered at the Washington 44; D'Andre Swift carried five times for 34 yards on the winning drive, including a 15-yard toss to the 18. [source]
  • Swift rushed for a then-season-high 108 yards on 19 carries and turned a short Williams pass into a 55-yard fourth-quarter touchdown. [source]
  • Williams, playing near his hometown and Gonzaga College High School, went 17-of-29 for 252 yards and a touchdown (98.6 rating) with no turnovers, and scored himself on a quarterback sweep that built a 13-0 lead. [source]
  • Jaquan Brisker picked off Daniels at the Bears' 2-yard line and returned it about 30 yards; the defense also recovered a Jacory Croskey-Merritt fumble and a Montez Sweat-forced fumble (T.J. Edwards) for three takeaways and three sacks. [source]

  • The win was Chicago's third straight and its second one-point road comeback in a row after the Raiders game; the Bears moved to 3-2. [source]

Sunday night preseason game (FOX national broadcast)

Bears postgame locker room | 2025 Preseason Week 2

How the Bears won

  • Caleb Williams played two series and went 6-of-10 for 107 yards and a touchdown (130.1 passer rating). His opening drive covered 92 yards in seven plays and ended with a 36-yard catch-and-run touchdown by Olamide Zaccheaus. [source]
  • Ben Johnson pulled the starters after Williams' second series; Buffalo sat its starters entirely and never picked up a first down against Chicago's first-team defense. [source]
  • Backup Tyson Bagent took over in the second quarter and threw an 11-yard touchdown to Tyler Scott; the Bears' own highlight package billed it as a 205-yard passing game for Bagent. [source]
  • Running back Ian Wheeler scored twice, on a 1-yard run that capped a 12-play drive for a 28-0 halftime lead and a 10-yard run that finished a 14-play, 86-yard fourth-quarter march for the final margin. [source]
  • Cairo Santos added a 40-yard field goal in the third quarter to make it 31-0. [source]
  • Buffalo cycled through Mike White, Mitchell Trubisky and Shane Buechele at quarterback; White and Trubisky combined to complete 11 of 24 passes for 110 yards, and Buffalo missed a 56-yard field goal and turned the ball over on downs. [source]
  • Rookie wideout Luther Burden III caught three passes for 49 yards, including a 22-yarder, and Zaccheaus' lone target went for the 36-yard score. [source]
  • Rookie pass rusher Austin Booker recorded his fourth sack of the preseason to stall a Bills drive. [source]
  • Cornerback Terell Smith suffered a knee injury, was carted off and ruled out; the shutout left the Bears 1-0-1 in the preseason and Buffalo 0-2. [source]

The chant

Chant No. 1 of the Johnson era and the first of the 2025 season.

  • This was the first time Johnson gathered the Bears in the locker room and led 'Good, better, best' — a saying he carried from his high school program at A.C. Reynolds in Asheville, N.C., where it was the post-win rallying cry. [source]
  • Per The Athletic's Kevin Fishbain, Kevin Byard recalled that when Johnson first did it after the Bills game, players were confused and looked around unsure what to do when told to repeat the lines back. [source]
  • Byard later admitted the saying struck him as a little corny at first ('I won't say it was corny, but it was... I don't really know'), and pinned the debut on the preseason Bills game. [source]
  • Johnson acknowledged some players 'looked at me like I was crazy the first time or two,' especially those who hadn't played in the preseason game; Cole Kmet summed it up as 'those things are cheesy until they're not.' [source]
  • The Bears did not release footage of the chant from this game; the first public look came after the Week 3 win over Dallas. [source]
